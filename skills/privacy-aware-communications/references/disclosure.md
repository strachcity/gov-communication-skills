# Disclosure

When a communication tells someone something about a person: the person themselves, someone acting for them, or a third party.

Last checked: 6 October 2026

Lines starting with ">" are quoted from the source. Lines starting with "Interpretation:" are ours.

There's no single cross-government disclosure policy on GOV.UK. Departments publish their own guidance. The 2 below apply the same principles from UK GDPR, so we use them as examples of how accountable organisations apply the law, not as rules for other organisations.

## Check who you're talking to

HMRC, "Information disclosure guide", IDG30230:

> Information about a customer should never be provided to a third party unless you are sure that there is lawful authority to disclose that information.

> When speaking on the telephone you must take every step to ensure that you do not disclose any HMRC information until you are wholly satisfied that the person you are speaking to is the person they claim to be.

HM Passport Office, "Data protection: caseworker guidance":

> You must only disclose personal customer data in line with the Disclosure of Information guidance or when other guidance allows you to.

> do not disclose personal customer data or special category data to colleagues or other departments who do not need to see it

Interpretation: before a call discusses a case, the person's identity is checked. A message that invites a call back should not reveal case detail, because the skill cannot know who will hear it or read it.

## Third parties and people acting for someone

HM Passport Office publishes separate guidance on consent: "Authorisation and consent: consent to disclose information".

Interpretation: whether a carer, relative or representative can be told about someone's case depends on the organisation's rules on authority and consent. Flag any message addressed to, or likely to be read by, someone other than the person. Never assume authority.

## Information about third parties

Interpretation: a message can reveal that a third party, like a doctor, an employer or a referee, is involved in a case. That may reveal special category information about the person, and something about the third party. For example, "We have contacted your GP" reveals a health matter.

Risk heuristic: saying more about a third party than the person needs to understand what's happening and what to do is high risk. Flag the level of detail for each channel. See `channels.md`.

## Identity checks

The government standard for checking someone's identity is "How to prove and verify someone's identity" (Good Practice Guide 45).

Interpretation: this skill doesn't design identity checks. It flags where a communication assumes the person has already been verified, like a call that discusses a case.
