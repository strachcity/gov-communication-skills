A grammar for case communication, not a folder of templates

A modifier changes how a moment is said, without being a moment of its own. Delay is the third.

We've made a decision
↓
Ask for feedback

A follow-up comes after a moment. It tells the customer nothing about their case, and never implies it affects it.

moment └variant modifier follow-up

Writing real patterns cut the list from 16 moments to 12, then 9. Every moment records its evidence, and none is marked tested with users yet.

Worked example

One pattern becoming a real communication

"We're waiting on someone else", adapted for an invented permit service.

Generic patternThe same for every service

We're waiting for information we need from [who we've asked] before we can continue with your [case].

You do not need to do anything now.

We'll update you by ((update date)).

CommunicationsOne version for each channel

Your permit application is waiting for information from someone else. You do not need to do anything. We'll update you by 18 October 2026.

Email

Dear Alex Morgan

We're waiting for information we need from someone else before we can continue with your permit application.

You do not need to do anything now.

What happens next
We'll update you by 18 October 2026, or sooner if we get what we need.

  • No invented timescaleThe update date comes from a confirmed commitment, not a guess at when the third party replies
  • No unapproved disclosureNeither version describes the third party. The text follows a confirmed decision
  • GOV.UK content guidance appliedWhat happens next and when, and "You do not need to do anything now"
Needs a service decision

Can the email say we're waiting on "the local council"?

It's recorded as a hypothesis, not a decision. The data protection officer decides. Until then, the email says "someone else".

Assurance

Reviewers look at one table, not every message

For a set of messages, the privacy skill lists what each one reveals in each channel. The answers become confirmed decisions in the service context.

  1. Patternsthe generic library
  2. Service contextthe service's facts and decisions
  3. Proposed messagesa draft for each moment and channel
  4. IA, data protection and policyreview the flagged rows
  5. Confirmed decisionsrecorded in the service context, with owner and date

  • The generic skills are reusableThe same plugin works for any government service, with no bespoke skill for each one
  • Service rules don't leak backThe service context is read, never written into the plugin. A rule becomes generic only by a recorded decision with a public source
  • It produces drafts, not sent messagesA service reviews them and loads them into a template tool, like GOV.UK Notify
  • It's tested, but not finishedThe content and privacy skills have blind-marked evals. The patterns and front door have had evals run once. No user testing yet
